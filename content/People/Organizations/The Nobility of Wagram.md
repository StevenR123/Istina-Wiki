Nobility in Wagram is and always has been a statement of wealth rather than divine right. Each of the noble houses bought and maintains a Noble Charter from the governing council that gives them the rights and responsibilities of nobility.

These Nobles are the only members of the city that have direct control of the governance of Wagram. To join the governing council one must be a noble.

Each house generally has a patron deity. Most houses have The [[Sanguine Blade]] as their patron.

# Levels of Nobility
## Knight
The lowest of Nobles.
The Knight Charter is the cheapest to purchase and their ranks are generally filled with prosperous merchants, prolific craftsmen, or Awakened adventurers. Often given to the lesser children of a noble house once the heir inherits their parents charter.

### Rights
Right to attend and speak at council meetings.
### Responsibilities
The cost to maintain the Charter is equal to the cost to purchase paid every 5 years.

## Knight

The lowest of Nobles.  
The Knight Charter is the cheapest to purchase, and their ranks are generally filled with prosperous merchants, prolific craftsmen, or Awakened adventurers. Often given to the lesser children of a noble house once the heir inherits their parent's charter.

### Rights

- Right to attend and speak at council meetings.

### Responsibilities

- The cost to maintain the Charter is equal to the cost to purchase, paid every 5 years.

---

## Lord

The first step into established nobility.  
Lords typically oversee small city districts and are expected to maintain a visible presence in their community. This rank is common for successful Knights seeking greater influence.

### Rights

- Right to attend and speak at council meetings.
- Priority status for minor business permits.

### Responsibilities

- Maintain a small contingent of soldiers for emergencies.
- Provide upkeep for a designated section of Wagram, such as a street or plaza.
- Charter maintenance cost paid every 4 years, slightly higher than Knights.

---

## Baron

The first rank to hold limited governance powers.  
Barons manage medium-sized districts and begin to take on more significant military and administrative responsibilities.

### Rights

- All Lord rights.
- Ability to propose minor legislation to the Council.
- Partial exemption from specific taxes.

### Responsibilities

- Maintain a moderate-sized force of trained soldiers.
- Oversee and govern a district, including its infrastructure and law enforcement.
- Charter maintenance cost paid every 3 years, moderately higher than Lords.

---

## Viscount

A higher-tier noble with notable influence.  
Viscounts manage large and vital districts of Wagram, often controlling key trade areas or cultural hubs.

### Rights

- All Baron rights.
- Increased legislative influence, including proposing major amendments.
- Full exemption from trade taxes for personal businesses.

### Responsibilities

- Maintain a substantial number of soldiers, including specialists such as mages or engineers.
- Ensure their district thrives economically and culturally.
- Charter maintenance cost paid every 3 years, significantly higher than Barons.

---

## Count

An elite noble rank with broad powers.  
Counts are the backbone of Wagram’s governance, directly shaping policies and handling large swaths of the city.

### Rights

- All Viscount rights.
- Ability to hold a voting seat in the Council.
- Access to exclusive noble privileges, including large estate ownership within the city.

### Responsibilities

- Maintain a large, fully equipped military force.
- Oversee multiple districts or a major urban center.
- Organize events or festivals to promote unity and prosperity.
- Charter maintenance cost paid every 2 years, significantly higher than Viscounts.

---

## Duke

The pinnacle of Wagram's nobility.  
Dukes are the most powerful nobles, wielding significant influence over the city’s direction. Only a handful of individuals hold this title at any time.

### Rights

- All Count rights.
- Hold a permanent Council seat.
- Authority to lead in times of crisis.
- Ownership rights over strategic city assets such as ports or markets.

### Responsibilities

- Maintain a standing army capable of independent operation.
- Act as stewards for the city’s most critical infrastructure.
- Lead initiatives for citywide improvements.
- Charter maintenance cost paid annually, at an extremely high rate
